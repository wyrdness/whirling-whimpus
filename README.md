## Overview

The Whirling Whimpus is a member of the 'fearsome critters' tradition — humorous tall-tale beasts told around the fires of late-19th- and early-20th-century North American logging camps. As described in William T. Cox's Fearsome Creatures of the Lumberwoods (1910) and Henry H. Tryon's Fearsome Critters (1939), the Whimpus has the head and body of a gorilla, enormous front feet, and the ability to spin in place at such speed that its body blurs out of sight, leaving only swirling dust and rustling leaves. It stations itself on a forest trail, spinning, and any animal or unwary woodsman that walks into the vortex is reduced to a thick syrup that drips down the trunk of a nearby tree. Like other fearsome critters (the hidebehind, agropelter, hodag, splinter cat) the Whimpus is a piece of camp-fire humor and hazing material rather than a sincere cryptid claim, but it has been documented in print since 1910 and remains a recognizable figure in regional folklore, especially in Tennessee and the Appalachian lumberwoods.

## Theories & Explanations

### Skeptical

**Lumberjack tall tale** _(Folklorists of the fearsome-critters tradition)_

The Whirling Whimpus is a piece of folkloric humour from American logging camps, used for entertainment and to haze newcomers. The 'invisible spinning beast' is a comic exaggeration of dust devils and forest noises rather than a claim about an unknown animal.
